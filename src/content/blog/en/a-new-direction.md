I want to start with the news that matters most to many of you: the demo of [HoldStrong](/holdstrong/) will not come out on 15 November. Unlike [last month](/blog/demo-moves-to-november/), I am not moving it to a new date this time. The demo is postponed until further notice, because I am going to change the direction of the game itself. In this post I want to explain how I came to that decision, what I intend to do now and what it means for the playtest.

## What the playtests showed me

Over the last few weeks the game has been played a lot, by me and by others, and the more of those sessions I watched, the clearer one uncomfortable conclusion became. HoldStrong, in the way it is currently put together, with its structure and its game loop, does not live up to what I want from it. The playtests showed that the game lacks fun, a real task and a clear goal. You hold the tower, you survive the day, and then the next day comes, but the reason why any of that should matter to you never quite arrives.

That is not a problem of balancing, and it is not something one more feature would fix. It is a sign that the game design itself was not executed well enough. That is exactly the point where I want to start, because tuning numbers on a foundation that does not carry would only cost me more time and still give you a game I would not be happy with.

## Where the mistake lies

This decision was not easy for me. I have put a lot of passion and a lot of time into HoldStrong, and some of what I built along the way I am still genuinely proud of. But I have to be honest here. Over the past months I built up a great deal of technical know-how, and you can see that in posts like [four days for one lightning strike](/blog/one-lightning-strike/), while I looked far too little at the big picture of the game design. I polished how things look and feel before I had made sure that the game underneath them is fun to play.

So the mistake is clearly on my side, and I do not want to hide it behind a vague announcement. The way I intend to make up for it is simple: I am going to try again, and this time I will start with the design.

## Where HoldStrong is heading

I am going to rethink and redesign the game design of HoldStrong from the ground up, and in doing so I will most likely end up with a new mix of genres. The goal is to turn HoldStrong into a turn-based strategy game with a certain twist. That is not just a vague wish. I already have concrete thoughts and ideas for it, and the first of them have turned out to be very promising, which is the main reason I feel confident enough to take this step at all.

The two pictures in this post come from that first prototype, and they are deliberately rough. The map is built from grey blocks and the enemies are still simple capsules, because this time I want to find out whether the game is fun before I spend a single day on how it looks, which is exactly the lesson of the last months. You lead a small group of heroes across a map, turn by turn, and each of them has action points for skills and movement points for walking. Before you set off, you pick a starting weapon for every hero, and that weapon decides their role and their skills. There is one detail I already like a lot: every hero comes with two traits that pull in different directions, like the hothead who lands more critical hits but aims worse. What the twist is, I will tell you once I know it holds up.

![The hero screen of the first prototype, November 2026 — "Choose the starting weapons" above three cards for Brida, Elsbeth and Roderick, each with a portrait, two traits such as Hothead, Sluggish or Tough, their stats and a row of six weapons to choose from](/blog/a-new-direction/prototype.webp)

At the same time, I do not want to pretend that this is a small adjustment. The character of the game will probably change, and that means big changes in many places. What exactly stays and what goes, I will show you here as soon as I am sure of it myself, rather than promising things today that might not survive the next few weeks.

## What happens to the demo and the playtest

To put it plainly once more, the demo will not come out on 15 November. What will come in the near future, though, is a playtest of the new direction, so that you can get your hands on it early and tell me whether it works. Publishing through Steam has turned out to be more difficult than I expected, so for the playtest I will most likely switch to a different platform. As soon as that is settled, I will announce it here in the devlog, together with how you can take part. Everyone who has already signed up for the playtest stays on the list, and if you want to join, <a href="/holdstrong/#demo" data-analytics="cta" data-analytics-id="join_playtest" data-analytics-location="section">you can still sign up here</a>.

Despite everything, I am really looking forward to what comes next, because for the first time I feel that the game is being built from the right end.
